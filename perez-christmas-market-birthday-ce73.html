Your trip at a glance

Three cities, one hotel in each

Robert and Marcela, this trip celebrates Marcela's 40th with twelve days in three of Europe's great Christmas cities. The route follows the market calendar, so you arrive in each city while its markets are open. Marcela's birthday on November 25 falls in Munich at the height of the Bavarian season, and the trip finishes in Prague on the weekend the Old Town Square market opens.

You travel between cities by direct train and stay in one hotel per city, so you unpack three times, not ten. Every hotel was chosen for its location first: each one is a short walk from that city's main market.

Day by day

Your twelve days

Departure and Zurich

Opening night of the Swiss markets
  1. Day 1Wed, Nov 18

    Depart Houston

    Your flight leaves Houston in the afternoon and connects overnight through Amsterdam to Zurich, with a checked bag and reserved seats for each of you.

    Departs 3:25 p.m.
  2. Day 2Thu, Nov 19

    Welcome to Zurich

    Your private driver meets you in arrivals and takes you to your hotel at the edge of the Niederdorf, Zurich's car-free Old Town. After a rest, step straight out into its lanes. Tonight is opening night for Zurich's Christmas markets, and cheese fondue at the Swiss Chuchi on Hirschenplatz is a short stroll away.

    Land 11:00 a.m.Private transfer
  3. Day 3Fri, Nov 20

    The Old Town and its markets

    Explore at your own pace. The Grossmünster, the Fraumünster, the Lindenhof lookout and the lakefront are all a short walk from the hotel. The markets sit within walking distance of one another: the large indoor market in the main train station, the Christmas village beside the Opera House, and the Singing Christmas Tree, where choirs perform each evening.

  4. Day 4Sat, Nov 21

    Day trip to Lucerne

    Lucerne is under an hour away by train. With a Swiss rail day pass you travel on your own schedule: walk the covered Chapel Bridge, browse the old town, stop at Lucerne's own Christmas market, and head back to Zurich whenever you are ready.

Munich

Four nights in Bavaria, birthday included
  1. Day 5Sun, Nov 22

    First-class train to Munich

    After breakfast you board the direct EuroCity, riding past Lake Constance and into Bavaria. Munich's markets close on this one Sunday each year for a local day of remembrance, which makes it the right day to travel. A short taxi ride takes you from the station to your hotel, and a relaxed Bavarian dinner nearby closes the day.

    Departs 11:32, arrives 15:16
  2. Day 6Mon, Nov 23

    Munich's Christkindlmarkt

    Today belongs to Munich's oldest and largest Christmas market, which fills Marienplatz and the streets in front of the New Town Hall. Catch the Glockenspiel, visit the Frauenkirche, and stop at the nativity market beside St. Peter's Church. The market is an easy walk from your hotel, so come back after dark when the lights are on.

  3. Day 7Tue, Nov 24

    Neuschwanstein and Hohenschwangau

    A small-group coach day into the Bavarian Alps to the royal castles of King Ludwig II. The tour leaves from Sonnenstraße, a ten-minute walk from your hotel, for the village of Hohenschwangau. You visit both Neuschwanstein, the castle that inspired Disney's Sleeping Beauty, and Hohenschwangau Castle, where Ludwig spent his childhood, with free time for lunch and photos against the mountains. You are back in Munich by evening.

    Departs 9:00 a.m.Castle entry $55 per person, paid at pickup
  4. Day 8Wed, Nov 25

    Happy birthday, Marcela

    Marcela's 40th falls in Munich, and the day is hers. Start slowly with a long breakfast, then spend the afternoon however she likes: the smaller markets around the city, the Residenz, or a return to Marienplatz to choose a keepsake ornament. In the evening I will reserve a birthday dinner in the Old Town, followed by a walk through the lit Christkindlmarkt for a glass of Glühwein.

Prague

Opening weekend on Old Town Square
  1. Day 9Thu, Nov 26

    Train to Prague

    The direct EuroCity crosses the Bavarian countryside and western Bohemia. A private driver meets you at the station and takes you to your hotel, tucked just off Old Town Square, about a minute's walk from the market and the Týn Church. Several of Prague's neighborhood markets are already open, so your first evening can be mulled wine and Czech treats.

    EC 357 departs 10:44, arrives 16:21Private transfer
  2. Day 10Fri, Nov 27

    Prague Castle and the Charles Bridge

    Cross the Charles Bridge to the Lesser Town and climb to Prague Castle and St. Vitus Cathedral for the view over the red rooftops, buying castle tickets on arrival and exploring at your own pace. A three-day transit pass covers the trams whenever your feet need a break. In the evening, the market at Peace Square is a local favorite and an easy tram ride from the hotel.

  3. Day 11Sat, Nov 28

    Old Town Square opens

    The Old Town Square Christmas market opens today, a minute from your hotel, with its giant tree set in front of the Týn Church. As evening falls the square fills with lights and music. Close the trip with a farewell dinner nearby and plan on an early night, since your driver arrives before dawn.

  4. Day 12Sun, Nov 29

    Journey home

    Your private driver collects you at the hotel for your morning flight, connecting through Paris and landing in Houston the same afternoon, with a checked bag and reserved seats for each of you.

Before you go

Travel documents and entry

Switzerland, Germany and the Czech Republic are all in the Schengen Area, so one set of rules covers the whole trip

U.S. passport

Valid through at least March 1, 2027 (three months past your November 29 departure), issued within the last ten years, with at least two blank pages each.

Visa

Not required. U.S. citizens may stay up to 90 days in any 180-day period. You clear passport control on arrival in Zurich and on departure from Prague.

Entry/Exit System (EES)

Europe's digital border system is in full use. Officers take your photo and fingerprints in Zurich in place of a passport stamp. Nothing to do in advance; allow a little extra time.

ETIAS

Not required for this trip and not yet in operation. Do not pay any website offering ETIAS applications.

Passports on the train

Germany is running police checks at its borders, including on trains from Switzerland and the Czech Republic. Keep passports on you, not in checked luggage, on both rail days.

Return ticket and funds

Border officers may ask for your return flight and proof you can cover your stay. Keep your itinerary and a credit card handy when you land.

Travel medical insurance

Strongly recommended. European hospitals generally do not accept U.S. health insurance, and Czech authorities may ask for proof of coverage. I will include a travel protection quote with your final booking.

Currency

Swiss francs in Zurich, euros in Munich, Czech koruna in Prague. Cards work almost everywhere; carry a little local cash for market stalls. Cash of 10,000 euros or more must be declared on entry.
