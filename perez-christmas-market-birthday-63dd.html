Day by day

Your twelve days

Open evenings for the markets, one guided day, and a birthday that belongs to Marcela

Day 1Wed, Nov 18Houston

Depart Houston

Your overnight flight leaves Houston for Zurich. Flight times will be added once your air is confirmed.

Day 2Thu, Nov 19Zurich

Welcome to Zurich

You land in the morning and take the short train ride from the airport into the city. Your hotel sits in the car-free Old Town, so after a rest you step straight out into the lanes of the Niederdorf. This is opening night for Zurich's Christmas markets, and dinner can be as easy as the hotel's own Swiss Chuchi restaurant, known for its cheese fondue.

Day 3Fri, Nov 20Zurich

Old Town and the markets

Explore at your own pace. The Grossmünster, the Fraumünster and the Lindenhof lookout are all a short walk from the hotel, as is the lakefront. The markets sit within walking distance of one another, from the large indoor market in the main train station to the Christmas village beside the Opera House and the Singing Christmas Tree, where choirs perform each evening.

Day 4Sat, Nov 21Lucerne

Day trip to Lucerne

Lucerne is under an hour away by train. Walk the covered Chapel Bridge, browse the old town and stop at Lucerne's own Christmas market before taking the train back to Zurich for the evening. Trains run frequently, so you come and go on your own schedule.

Day 5Sun, Nov 22Zurich to Munich

First-class train to Munich

Departs 11:32 · Arrives 15:16

After breakfast you board the direct EuroCity from Zurich, riding past Lake Constance and into Bavaria. Munich's markets close on this one Sunday each year for a local day of remembrance, which makes it the right day to travel. Settle into your hotel and enjoy a relaxed Bavarian dinner nearby.

Day 6Mon, Nov 23Munich

Munich's Christkindlmarkt

Today belongs to Munich's oldest and largest Christmas market, which fills Marienplatz and the surrounding streets in front of the New Town Hall. Catch the Glockenspiel, visit the Frauenkirche and stop at the nativity market beside St. Peter's Church. The market is an easy walk from your hotel, so you can return in the evening when the lights are at their best.

Day 7Tue, Nov 24Bavarian Alps

Neuschwanstein Castle

9:15 a.m. to about 6:30 p.m.

A guided full-day trip in English to Neuschwanstein, the castle King Ludwig II built into the Alpine foothills. Your guide handles all of the transportation and tells the story of the king and his castles along the way, with time for the view from the Marienbrücke and the village of Hohenschwangau. Entry to the castle interior is purchased on the morning of the tour and depends on that day's availability.

Day 8Wed, Nov 25Munich

Happy Birthday, Marcela

Marcela's 40th falls in Munich, and the whole day is hers. Start slowly with a long breakfast, then spend the afternoon however she likes: the smaller markets around the city, the Residenz, or a return to Marienplatz to choose a keepsake ornament to mark the occasion. In the evening I will reserve a birthday dinner in the Old Town, followed by a walk through the lit Christkindlmarkt for a glass of Glühwein.

Day 9Thu, Nov 26Munich to Prague

Train to Prague

Departs 10:44 · Arrives 16:21

The direct train crosses the Bavarian countryside and western Bohemia. Your hotel sits right on Old Town Square, steps from the Astronomical Clock. Several of Prague's neighborhood markets are already open, so your first evening can be mulled wine and Czech treats.

Day 10Fri, Nov 27Prague

Prague Castle and Charles Bridge

Cross the Charles Bridge to the Lesser Town and climb to Prague Castle and St. Vitus Cathedral for the view over the red rooftops. In the evening, the market at Peace Square is a local favorite and an easy tram ride from your hotel.

Day 11Sat, Nov 28Prague

Old Town Square opening

The Old Town Square Christmas market opens today, right outside your hotel, with its giant tree in front of the Týn Church. As evening falls the square fills with lights and music, a fitting last night in Europe, with a farewell dinner nearby to close out the trip.

Day 12Sun, Nov 29Prague

Journey home

After breakfast, a transfer takes you to Prague's airport for your flight home to Houston.

Before you go

Travel documents and entry

Switzerland, Germany and the Czech Republic are all in Europe's Schengen Area, so one set of rules covers the whole trip. As U.S. citizens you do not need a visa for a stay of this length. You pass through passport control when you land in Zurich and again when you leave from Prague.

U.S. passportValid for at least three months after you leave Europe on November 29 (so through at least March 1, 2027), issued within the last ten years, with at least two blank pages each.
VisaNot required. U.S. citizens may visit the Schengen Area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period.
Entry/Exit System (EES)Europe's digital border system is in full use. In Zurich, officers take your photo and fingerprints instead of stamping your passport. Nothing to do in advance, but allow a little extra time.
ETIASNot required for this trip. ETIAS is Europe's upcoming online travel authorization and is not yet in operation. Do not pay any website offering ETIAS applications.
Return ticket and fundsOfficers may ask to see your return flight and proof you can cover your stay. Keep your itinerary and a credit card handy when you land.
Passports on the trainGermany is running police checks at its borders, including on trains from Switzerland and the Czech Republic. Keep passports on you, not in checked bags, on both rail days.
Travel medical insuranceStrongly recommended. European hospitals generally do not accept U.S. health insurance, and Czech authorities may ask visitors for proof of travel medical coverage. I will provide a travel protection quote with your final booking.
CurrencySwiss francs in Zurich, euros in Munich and Czech koruna in Prague. Cards are accepted almost everywhere; carry a little local cash for market stalls. Cash of €10,000 or more must be declared on entry.
