Planned around the two of you

What I kept in mind for you both

A comfortable pace

No day here asks for more than a half day of touring, and none of them starts before breakfast. Each arrival day is left free so you can settle in after the flights, and the afternoon before every early departure is kept light so you can pack and get to bed at a reasonable hour.

Quito's altitude

Quito sits at about 9,350 feet, and you land there in the evening after two flights, with an early flight to the Galápagos two mornings later. Your one day in the city is a private half day by car with short walks between stops, so you see the old town without wearing yourselves out. I left out the TelefériQo cable car on purpose, since it climbs to nearly 13,000 feet. Drink plenty of water, go easy on alcohol that first night, and take the stairs slowly.

Boats, landings and steps

Several days involve stepping into a small boat or onto a beach from one. The crews do this every day and will give you a hand in and out, and a pair of water shoes with a good grip makes it much easier. In the Galápagos you choose each landing for yourselves, and I have marked which ones are easy and which ones are steep or rocky so Patricia can decide which ones to do and which to enjoy from the boat.

Mondays, checked

Two of your days fall on a Monday, when much of Panama City's old town and Costa Rica's Manuel Antonio National Park are closed. I planned around both, so your Panama City morning focuses on the streets and plazas that do not depend on museum hours, and your Quepos day goes to the mangroves instead of the park.

Panama CityEasy

A private morning in Casco Viejo

Monday, February 1
About 3 hours, mid-morning start Starts at your hotel door

Why this is right for your first full day

Your hotel sits on Plaza de la Independencia, in the middle of the old quarter, so the best of Panama City starts right outside the front door. A private guide walks you through the plazas and restored colonial streets at your speed, out to Plaza de Francia and along the seawall promenade, where you look across the bay to the modern skyline. A car stays with you the whole morning, so whenever Patricia is ready to sit down, you ride to the next stop instead of walking it.

Monday is when the old town's museums close, so I built this morning around the streets, churches and views rather than museum visits. You finish with lunch at a café in the old town and have the afternoon free for the hotel pool or a nap before boarding the next morning.

Private guide and car, for the two of you.
Tuesday, February 2, and Wednesday, February 3

Boarding Star Legend and the canal transit

Your driver collects you at 9:00 a.m. for the ride to Colón, and the ship sails that evening. The canal transit is the reason for this cruise, so there is nothing to book. Find a spot on the forward deck as you approach the locks, and ask about the Open Bridge, where Windstar lets guests watch the captain and crew guide the ship through.

Fuerte AmadorEasy

The Gamboa rainforest aerial tram

Thursday, February 4
A half day, back on board by mid-afternoon Soberanía National Park, beside the canal

Why this is the best fit of the cruise

After a city day and a canal day, this gives you the rainforest, and you see it sitting down. An open-air gondola carries you up through the forest canopy, rising about 280 feet over half a mile with a naturalist pointing out birds, sloths and howler monkeys along the way. At the top, an observation tower looks out over the Chagres River and the canal you sailed through the day before.

The tower is optional. The walk to it takes about two minutes, and the climb is a gentle ramp rather than stairs, so Patricia can decide on the spot. Before or after the ride there is time for the resort's orchid and butterfly exhibits. I will book this through the ship or privately, whichever gives you the better day.

Round-trip transportation from the pier, tram ride and guide.
Friday, February 5, at sea, and Saturday, February 6, Isla Parida

Two slower days

The day at sea is a good morning for the World Spa, and treatment times on sea days fill first, so book as soon as you are aboard. At Isla Parida, keep the day open. If the beach landing looks easy that morning, go ashore. Otherwise, the ship's marina platform is right at water level, and Wes can take out a kayak or paddleboard or go snorkeling while Patricia reads on deck.

Golfo Dulce, Costa RicaEasy

Casa Orquídeas botanical garden

Sunday, February 7
A half-day visit Reached by boat across the gulf

Why I chose this over a rainforest hike

Golfo Dulce is one of the wildest corners of Costa Rica, and most of what it offers is a hike through humid jungle. Casa Orquídeas brings that same forest to you on garden paths. A family-run botanical garden at the edge of the rainforest, reachable only by water, it holds orchids, heliconias, gingers and palms from across the tropics, and toucans and tanagers come in to feed while you walk.

The visit is a leisurely guided stroll with plenty of places to pause. The one step to plan for is getting in and out of the boat, and the crew will help you both.

Boat transfer, garden entry and guided walk.
Quepos, Costa RicaEasy

A mangrove estuary by boat

Monday, February 8
A half day Seated, shaded boat

Why the mangroves, and not the national park

Manuel Antonio National Park is the famous name here, but it closes every Monday, and Monday is your day in Quepos. The mangroves around Isla Damas are the better choice for you anyway. You sit in a covered boat while a guide takes you through the channels at low speed, and the wildlife comes to the water's edge: white-faced monkeys, crocodiles, herons and kingfishers, with the occasional sloth in the branches overhead.

There is almost no walking, the shade keeps the midday heat off, and you are back at the ship with the afternoon free.

Transportation from the pier, boat tour and naturalist guide.
Tuesday, February 9

Arrive in San José

San José, Costa RicaEasy

Doka Estate coffee tour

Wednesday, February 10
A morning tour, back by early afternoon On the slopes of Poás Volcano

Why this suits your last day in Costa Rica

Doka is a working coffee estate run by the same family since the 1940s, a short drive from your hotel. February falls in the harvest season, so you see the coffee being picked and processed, not just hear about it. The tour moves from the plants to the drying patios and the roasting room, and it ends with a tasting, so you go home knowing exactly what makes Costa Rican coffee good.

I planned this for the morning on purpose. Your flight leaves at 9:30 the next morning, and this way you are back at the hotel with the afternoon to pack. I passed on the volcano and waterfall day trips because both mean long hours in the car and a lot of stairs.

Round-trip transportation from your hotel, guided tour and tasting.
Quito, EcuadorEasy to moderate

A private half day in Quito's historic center

Friday, February 12
3 to 4 hours, late-morning start About 9,350 feet above sea level

Why a private car matters here

Quito's colonial center is one of the best preserved in the Americas, and the way to see it at altitude is with your own driver and guide. You ride between the stops and walk only the short stretches worth walking: Plaza Grande with the cathedral and the presidential palace, the gold-covered interior of La Compañía church, and the hilltop viewpoint at El Panecillo, which looks over the whole city and the volcanoes around it.

The late start gives you a slow breakfast after the previous evening's flights, and you are back at the hotel by mid-afternoon to rest before the early flight to the islands.

Private guide and car, for the two of you.

February 13 to 20, aboard Galaxy Orion

The Galápagos, landing by landing

Every landing and snorkel stop is already included with your cruise, led by the ship's naturalist guides. Nothing here needs booking. What helps is knowing ahead of time which landings are gentle and which ones ask more, so you can each choose your days. February is the warm season in the islands, with calmer seas and pleasant water for anyone who wants to snorkel from the beach.

Easy landings, do not miss these

  • Bachas Beach, Santa Cruz, your first afternoon
  • Cerro Brujo, San Cristóbal, a long white beach with sea lions
  • The San Cristóbal highlands, with giant tortoises in the open
  • Lobos Island, for sea lions and blue-footed boobies
  • Gardner Bay, Española, one of the islands' finest beaches
  • Post Office Bay, Floreana, where you can leave a postcard in the sailors' barrel
  • The Charles Darwin Research Station, your final morning

Landings that ask more

  • Bartolomé, a boardwalk of several hundred wooden steps to the island's famous viewpoint
  • Sullivan Bay, Santiago, on uneven lava
  • Pitt Point, San Cristóbal, steep and rocky in places
  • Suárez Point, Española, the longest and rockiest walk of the week, with the albatross and booby colonies as the reward

On these, Patricia can go at her own pace, turn back early with a guide, or stay with the boat. Your naturalist will tell you each evening what the next day's landing involves.
